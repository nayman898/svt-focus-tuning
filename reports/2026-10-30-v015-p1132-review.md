## Interpretation

The sequence is consistent with delayed or invalid early narrowband feedback
aggravating an already-rich startup: a low reported signal is followed by fuel
addition and greater measured richness, then rich feedback, fuel removal and
fallback. This is evidence to test sensor readiness, heater operation and wiring,
not proof that the sensor itself failed. An unsuitable closed-loop readiness
calibration or incorrect wideband measurement remains possible.

The later rich narrowband signal often agrees with the rich wideband. It should
not be called false merely because P1132 is present. Ford describes a
lack-of-switching rich fault; sensor trouble and an actual fuel-system shift can
both produce lack of switching. The cited Ford summary is general gasoline
monitor context, not verified UJAHAS3 thresholds or an SVT wiring procedure.

Earlier v006 and v014 reviews also recorded delayed upstream O2 response during
rich startup. Repetition raises the priority of checking the feedback hardware.
The v014 open-loop experiment still developed idle dips, however, so this finding
does not establish one cause for every historical stumble. Warm near-stoichiometric
operation in this run also argues against a blanket global fuel subtraction.

The [pressure/injector audit](2026-10-30-v015-pressure-injector-audit.md) found
neutral exposed injector pressure multipliers. Pump bypass and desired-pressure
table labels do not demonstrate a changing injector correction. Actual injector
pulse width and independent differential pressure are still missing.

After about 396 s, throttle and RPM change substantially. There is no vehicle
speed, load or injector pulse-width channel in this recording. Mixed rich and
very lean wideband readings in that section cannot be turned into a cruise/boost
fuel correction without separating operating states. No driving clearance is
established by this analysis.

## Next diagnostic step

1. With the engine off and exhaust cool, identify the upstream O2 part number,
   location, any extension/splices, connector condition and routing near heat.
   Inspect nearby exhaust joints for leaks. Preserve the current fault evidence.
2. Using the exact 2004 SVT wiring diagram and sensor specifications, measure
   heater supply, control and current from cold through warm. A scan-tool heater
   command alone cannot establish that the heater works. Do not apply generic
   pin numbers or resistance limits from another Ford application.
3. Compare the physical sensor signal and signal-return integrity with the
   PCM-reported voltage using suitable high-impedance diagnostic equipment.
   A technician with a scope can separate a sensor/heater problem from a harness
   or acquisition problem more effectively than another uninstrumented idle run.
4. If the signal path checks out, validate wideband installation/measurement,
   then investigate actual startup overfueling using injector identification,
   pulse width and pressure relative to manifold pressure. Do not arbitrarily
   lower regulator pressure or subtract startup fuel from these logs alone.

The prepared Heater State profile can supplement this test, but its optional
channels still require a short coverage check. No engine run is started here.
Keep the test attended and stop for worsening stumbling; do not wait for a stall
or a fan cycle to obtain a complete recording.

If heater/signal testing fails, repair and repeat the same v015 comparison. If
hardware is verified and closed loop demonstrably starts before usable feedback,
audit the readiness settings against the strategy before preparing a single-change
candidate. No numeric tune change or v016 is selected from this capture.
